357   1 Conventions
358   The key words "MUST", "MUST NOT", "REQUIRED", "SHALL", "SHALL NOT", "SHOULD",
359   "SHOULD NOT", "RECOMMENDED", "NOT RECOMMENDED", "MAY", and "OPTIONAL" in
360   this document are to be interpreted as described in BCP14 [RFC2119] [RFC8174] when, and
361   only when, they appear in all capitals, as shown here.
362
363   The tables in this document are normative. The figures and notes in this document are
364   informative.
365
366   Referenced document titles are written as LoRaWAN MAC Layer Protocol Specification and
367   referenced section titles within this document are written as “Physical Layer”.
368
369   The protocol commands discussed in this document are called MAC commands, they are
370   written NewChannelReq, Bits, bit fields, and session attributes are written CFListType,
371   constants are written RECEIVE_DELAY1, variables are written N.
372   In this document:
373       • The octet order for all multi-octet fields SHALL be little endian.
374      • By default, RFU bits are Reserved for Future Use and SHALL be set to 0 by the
375        transmitter of the frame and SHALL be silently discarded by the receiver.
376      • A range of integer values is noted as min_value-max_value, [min_value:max_value], or
377        [min_value to max_value] where minimum and maximum values are included.
378      • Random numbers SHALL be the output of either a true random generator, or a pseudo-
379        random number generator that is seeded differently for each end-device and on each
380        re-initialization. Random(A-B) is a uniformly distributed random variable within range A-
381        B.
382      • floor() and ceil() are respectively the floor and ceiling functions.
383      • The concatenation operator is noted |. Concatenation with an empty list of octets leaves

385   2 Introduction
386
387   This document describes the LoRaWAN regional parameters for different regulatory regions
388   worldwide. This document is a companion document to the various versions of the LoRaWAN
389   MAC Layer Protocol Specification [TS001]. Separating the regional parameters from the
390   protocol specification allows addition of new regions to the former document without impacting
391   the latter document.
392
393              NOTICE: LoRa Alliance provides all information herein in its capacity
394              as a stakeholder in the promotion and adoption of secure, carrier-grade
395              IoT LPWAN connectivity, and in the context of LoRa Alliance’s
396              LoRaWAN standard. This information does not constitute technical or
397              legal advice and should not be relied upon to determine regulatory
398              compliance in any given jurisdiction. Each user of the information is
399              responsible for its use of the information, including the need to verify
400              current information and its applicability to the user.
401
402
403   The goal of the LoRa Alliance Regional Parameters Working Group is to allow the adoption of
404   the LoRaWAN technology across as many regions of the world as possible. It is to the benefit
405   of the entire eco-system to minimize the number of distinct channel plan definitions. By re-
406   using existing channel plans for regulatory regions that are newly adopting LoRaWAN, the
407   overall effort and the time required for products to be available in the new market is minimized.
408   The Regional Parameters Working Group welcomes and encourages collaboration with local
409   companies and regulators when LoRaWAN is considered for introduction to a new region.
410
411   This document defines regional parameters described in all LoRaWAN Link Layer
412   Specifications published prior to the publication of this document. Where differences exist
413   between LoRaWAN Link Layer specification revisions, they are highlighted in the text.
414
415   Where various attributes of a transmission signal are stated with regard to a region or
416   regulatory environment, this document is not intended to be an authoritative source of regional
417   governmental requirements or regulations and we refer the reader to the specific laws and
418   regulations of the country or region in which they desire to operate in order to obtain
419   authoritative information.
420
421   It must be noted here that, regardless of the parameters specified or configured, at no time is
422   any LoRaWAN equipment allowed to operate in a manner contrary to the prevailing local rules
423   and regulations where it is operating. It is the responsibility of the LoRaWAN end-device or
424   gateway to ensure that compliant operation is maintained without any outside assistance from
425   a LoRaWAN network or any other external mechanism.

2090    5 Physical Layer
2091
2092    The LoRaWAN uses a physical layer to communicate with other devices. Three physical
2093    layers are currently supported through the long range (LoRa), Long-Range Frequency
2094    Hopping Spread Spectrum (LR-FHSS), and Frequency shift keying (FSK) modulations.

2095    5.1 LoRa Description

2096    5.1.1 LoRa Packet Physical Structure
2097    LoRa uplink and downlink messages use the radio packet explicit header mode, in which the
2098    LoRa physical header (PHDR) plus a header CRC (PHDR_CRC) are included. In explicit header
2099    mode, the PHDR specifies the payload length in bytes, the forward error correction rate, and
2100    the presence of an OPTIONAL CRC for the payload. The integrity of the payload is protected
2101    by a CRC for uplink messages. LoRaWAN beacons are transmitted using LoRa modulation in
2102    implicit header mode with no payload CRC. The size of the payload depends on the beacon’s
2103    data rate. In implicit header mode, neither the PHDR nor PHDR_CRC are present.
2104    The PHDR, PHDR_CRC and payload CRC fields are inserted by the radio transceiver.

2169   5.3 LR-FHSS Description
2170   LR-FHSS modulation is only used on the uplink.

2171   5.3.1 LR-FHSS Physical Layer Description
2172   LR-FHSS is a fast frequency hopping spread spectrum (FHSS) modulation with bit rates
2173   ranging from 162bits/s to 325bits/s.
2174   When a device transmits a packet using LR-FHSS on a given channel, the packet content is
2175   modulated across several pseudo-random frequencies that spans the interval:
2176                                   𝐹𝑖𝑛𝑡𝑒𝑟𝑣𝑎𝑙 = 𝑐𝑒𝑛𝑡𝑟𝑒𝑓𝑟𝑒𝑞 ± 𝑏𝑤/2
2177   For FCC 47 CFR Part 15 compliance, the end-device frequency hops across 60 physical
2178   channels on a 25.4 kHz frequency grid.
2179   For ETSI based countries, the end-device frequency hops across 35 or 86 physical channels
2180   on a 3.9 kHz frequency grid.
2181   All physical channels are statistically used equally.
2182   The transmission starts on a random frequency inside the interval, and the following frequency
2183   hopping pattern is also randomly selected and announced in the LR-FHSS packet physical
2184   header. The transmission carrier frequency changes every 102.4 msec for each payload
2185   fragment, and 233.472 msec for each PHY header.
2186   The instantaneous LR-FHSS modulation bandwidth is 488 Hz. Therefore, a single LR-FHSS
2187   channel actually corresponds to many physical frequency channels.
2188   The LR-FHSS frequency hopping bandwidth is region-specific.
