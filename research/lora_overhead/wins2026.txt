  Overall, the results highlight a fundamental trade-off be-
tween network scalability, energy efficiency, and latency.
While deeper multi-hop routes and higher traffic loads enable
broader coverage and more frequent data reporting, they
impose additional energy and delay costs. These findings em-
phasize the importance of traffic-aware routing strategies and
adaptive reporting mechanisms to balance performance and
energy consumption in infrastructure monitoring deployments.

                           VI. C ONCLUSION
   This paper evaluated the performance of a multi-hop LoRa
mesh network deployed over a linear bridge infrastructure
using a custom simulation framework. Two operating scenarios
were examined: baseline monitoring with low reporting fre-
quency and increased traffic load with higher reporting rates.
Performance was assessed in terms of packet delivery ratio,
energy consumption, and end-to-end latency for nodes located
at different hop distances from the sink.
   The results demonstrate that the evaluated mesh-based LoRa
architecture enables communication beyond the limitations of
single-hop LoRa, achieving high packet delivery ratios under
baseline conditions and maintaining acceptable performance
under increased traffic loads. While energy consumption and
latency increase with hop count and traffic intensity, these
effects scale predictably and remain suitable for delay-tolerant
infrastructure monitoring applications. The findings confirm
that cooperative multi-hop forwarding is a viable solution for
infrastructure-constrained and delay-tolerant IoT deployments.
   Future work will focus on integrating adaptive routing
metrics, traffic-aware scheduling mechanisms, and energy-
balancing strategies to further enhance network scalability and
robustness under dynamic operating conditions.
